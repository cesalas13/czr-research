# Diligence Memo: HYPERIUM (HYP, agent-lane token on Robinhood chain)

**Summary:** Paper long on a verified-catalyst thesis; stopped for -$128 (-26 percent) on the $500 line while a smaller earlier tranche round-tripped a +48 percent gain to flat. Graded Reasoning B, Outcome F; the durable lesson is that regime gate outranks lore tier, and that verification artifacts must be reproducible.

## Asset and market context

HYPERIUM appeared on my scanner around 11:30am on July 12, 2026 at a $1.78M market cap, up 648 percent on the hour with $139k of liquidity, paired against VIRTUAL. It presented as a Virtuals-ecosystem AI agent token landing during a confirmed agent-lane revival on the chain, exactly the vacancy my doctrine had been waiting for: no recognizable Virtuals agent had deployed on-chain yet, and the first verified one was designated the starting gun. All positions below were paper positions on a paper desk.

## Mechanism / how it works

Later contract work showed HYP, like the other agent-lane tokens, was an EIP-1167 proxy of the same verified AgentTokenV4 implementation from the same factory, listed in the official Virtuals API. So classic honeypot mechanics were unlikely; the risk was always product substance, not contract mechanics. Supporting evidence at entry time: LP compounding from $116k to $170k, the healthiest young-coin liquidity trend of that weekend.

## Holder base and flow evidence

Entry discipline held initially: mid-vertical means no entry, so the desk armed a retrace watch instead of buying the spike. The aggressive book took a $250 dabble at $1.76M on a funnel-confirmed wave-2. The balanced book waited for verification. At roughly 4:45pm the verification loop closed: the desk logged HYPERIUM as recognized by the Virtuals account, upgraded it to R3-eligible, and placed a $500 resting bid (regime-halved) in the $2.25M to $2.35M zone, stop below $1.75M, scale at $4M.

## Risks and invalidation

Written risks: young ungated token, thin tape, and the whole thesis resting on one verification artifact. Invalidation was the $1.75M stop. What I underweighted: the chain regime. The anchor was cycling through warning and deepbreak that weekend, and my own book was simultaneously accumulating evidence that satellite bids during anchor cascades get run over.

## Decision taken and sizing logic

$500 on the balanced book (half-max, regime-halved) filled in the $2.25M to $2.35M zone; $250 dabble on the aggressive book from $1.78M. Both sized correctly per the meter. The sizing was not the error.

## Outcome and post-mortem

The R4 dabble marked +48 percent at $2.64M and later spiked toward $3.35M without ever tagging the $4M scale; the gain went unharvested, the second such miss that day, and it fed the desk's now-mandatory ratchet-after-scale rule. The R3 position stopped at $1.75M on July 13 for about -$128 on $500, roughly -26 percent, filled one leg too high. Book grade as written: thesis A-quality (verified agent, vacancy catalyst, compounding LP), Reasoning B, Outcome F, with the day's theme on record: verified-lore names still round-trip in a deepbreak regime; regime gate beats lore tier.

Then the thesis itself failed diligence. A primary-source research pass found the Virtuals listing had been created on July 12 itself, the product was a waitlist-only developer tool with no public team or GitHub, and the claimed official recognition post could not be found, conflicting with the desk's own verification entry. HYP was downgraded to costume-risk pending a reproducible artifact. A later repo-safety review of the project's published code found a five-hour-old single-dump history with a self-updater and stored-secret access, ruled not safe to run. The token eventually printed $86K in the desk's full-universe scan: vacancy thesis dead at the token level.

Was the reasoning right? Partially. Waiting for verification instead of chasing the vertical was correct process. But I accepted a verification artifact I could not later reproduce, and I let a lore upgrade override a live regime downgrade. Both failure modes are now written rules.
