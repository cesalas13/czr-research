# Diligence Memo: HOPPY (Robinhood chain memecoin)

**Summary:** Paper long at $1.05M market cap on a volatility thesis, exited in two pre-planned scales for +$192 (+77% blended) inside three hours. Graded Outcome A, Reasoning A; the book's model trade, and the asset later went to zero anyway.

## Asset and market context

HOPPY was a mascot-lane memecoin on the Robinhood chain, trading around a $1M to $1.3M market cap when I took the position on July 12, 2026. Context matters here: my desk runs a regime gate keyed to the chain's anchor coin (CASHCAT), and the anchor was in WARNING that morning, which under desk rules halves all new long sizes and restricts entries to resting limit orders. This was a paper position on a paper desk. The book opened that day with $10,000 of notional equity and an explicit mandate to trade its own doctrine.

## Mechanism / how it works

No yield mechanism; HOPPY was a plain attention asset. The tradeable property was behavioral: the coin moved violently in both directions on thin liquidity. My thesis, written before entry, was to monetize that violence rather than predict direction: place a resting bid well under spot, and if it fills on a whip, ship pre-placed scale-outs and a stop at open.

## Holder base and flow evidence

The entry trigger was flow, not narrative. HOPPY slid 22 percent off its $1.26M morning high within a session, the kind of air pocket the violent-both-ways profile produces on schedule. Later evidence confirmed the holder base was a rotation crowd: on July 14 the desk logged HOPPY tape at 403 buys versus 413 sells through a 38 percent hourly move, parity uniques through big moves being the farmed-range tell that holders are the day traders.

## Risks and invalidation

Primary risk was that the dip was the start of a real collapse, not a whip. Invalidation was priced, not argued: stop below $0.75M, written at entry, no renegotiation permitted under book rules. Position size was $250, the smallest tier, sized for regime warning and for a coin whose hourly range could eat a normal stop.

## Decision taken and sizing logic

Resting bid at $1.05M filled around 11:05am on the slide (effective $1.06M with the book's 1 percent slippage assumption). Scale-outs were pre-placed at open: half off through $1.6M, runner target $2.2M, stop below $0.75M.

## Outcome and post-mortem

Both exits printed as written. At 12:10pm a $1.71M print cleared the $1.6M level: half banked for +$61 (+49 percent), and per doctrine the stop ratcheted to breakeven. At 1:40pm a $2.27M print cleared the $2.2M target and the runner closed for +$131. Trade total +$192, +77 percent blended, on a round trip of $1.26M to $985k to $1.71M inside 90 minutes.

Grade on the book: Outcome A, Reasoning A. Two things make this the model trade rather than a lucky one. First, it validated the day's structural pattern: the book's resting structural entries earned +$332 gross that day while its market-order confirmation chases lost $214 gross. Second, the discipline held after the exit. HOPPY ran to $2.71M immediately after my runner closed, and on July 14 it broke to a $4.56M all-time high. The desk fired its ATH watch line, checked uniques (1.06 to 1.37 across frames, below the 1.5 confirmation bar), recognized a banned chase shape, and wrote a conditional re-entry card instead of buying. That card never triggered. HOPPY subsequently collapsed and shows in the desk's later full-universe scan in the sub-$100K graves tier.

Was the reasoning right? Yes, twice: the entry monetized the exact behavior the thesis named, and the refusal to chase the ATH avoided riding the asset to zero. Disciplined exits left roughly $500k of market cap on the table in the short run and saved the entire position in the long run. That asymmetry is the point of the process.
