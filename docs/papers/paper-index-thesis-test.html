2. Executive Summary

What it is. INDEX is a fixed-supply token on Robinhood Chain. Holders get paid in tokenized stocks.

How the payment works. A fee hook on its Uniswap v4 pool takes a cut of every trade in native ETH. A separate contract spends that ETH on tokenized AAPL, NVDA, TSLA and others, then sends them to holders.

The bet. Trading activity on a new chain funds a dividend stream. More activity means more dividends. More dividends attract more holders.

The tension this paper resolves. The thesis is coherent and the machinery is real. The measured inflow that funds it has fallen about 86 percent in nine days.

The evidence. Daily holder inflow ran about $18,300 per day on 2026-07-24 and about $2,600 per day by 2026-08-02. Payout yield per $1,000 held fell from a healthy median near $2.55 per day to $0.875 per day. Two measurement methods disagree on level and agree on direction. Only 14.2 percent of INDEX volume touches the pool that actually pays holders.

The verdict split. Of five bull legs, four hold in mechanism but fail on scale, and one is an unverified narrative.

The lifecycle call. Plateau to early unwind, meaning activity has flattened and has begun falling while the narrative keeps selling. Newer product lines still function. The core yield engine is decaying.

The standing desk read. Exit stands, watch-only below a $10M market cap. This paper does not overturn that read. It explains exactly what would.

The trigger list. Holder inflow above $10,000 per day sustained a week. Hook share above 50 percent. Payout yield at or above $2.55 per day. Pons cohort survival, the share of one week's launched tokens still trading a week later, above 15 percent. At least one new stock added to a basket in a trailing week.

What this paper is not. It sets no exit level and projects no return. It is a framework and an evidence test.

Read it top down. The two branches under the chain are two separate companies, and the only edge that crosses between them is Sinjoh's burn route at the bottom. Every claim that walks up one branch and down the other, Pons launch volume standing in for INDEX holder yield, is crossing a boundary that does not exist on-chain.

Layer 1: INDEX, the token and its fee hook

INDEX (0x56910D4409F3a0C78C64DD8D0545FF0705389870) is a fixed-supply 1 billion ERC20. The token contract itself does no fee math. It holds a holder registry, adding and removing wallets at a 10,000 INDEX threshold on every transfer, with the on-chain minShareBalance reading exactly 10,000e18.

The fee lives outside the token, in a Uniswap v4 hook (IndexFeeHook, 0x2cD91bD228ff4c537031d6b8204782090c84c0cC) attached to one specific pool, the INDEX/ETH v4 pool with poolId 0x00dd2df2f17d431cf3a0938f06c9cf9abc5e9643b6cc466ca3f71f3af246edf3. The hook takes its cut in native ETH on the way in and on the way out. A distributor contract then market-buys a basket of tokenized stocks (AAPL, NVDA, TSLA, MSFT and others, sourced through Rialto, a third-party tokenized-stock venue on the same chain, per memos/memo-index.md) and pays registered holders on a keeper cycle the site advertises as every 15 minutes. A keeper is an automated caller, a bot with permission to trigger the contract's payout step on a schedule, since contracts cannot run themselves.

Two fee numbers appear in this ecosystem and they are not the same number. The pool's own swap fee is 1.00 percent, read directly off the pool's Initialize event (fee = 0x2710 = 10000 parts per million). The 3 percent figure that appears throughout this paper is the modeled hook take applied to holder inflow. The two differ because the pool fee is what a swapper pays the pool, while the hook's holder cut is a separate transfer that Dune does not decode. Every holder-inflow figure below is therefore modeled at 3 percent, not measured at 3 percent, and that disclosure sits on the live board as well.

The disclosed admin powers matter for section 4's third leg: the owner can change supported assets, change keeper permissions (which bot is allowed to trigger payouts), and change the payout asset. These are documented in the project's own materials and confirmed against the contract in memos/memo-index.md.

Layer 2: Pons, a separate launchpad

Pons is a token launchpad on the same chain. It is a different project from INDEX, with different contracts and different deployers. Its launch factories are PonsLaunchFactory (0xA5aAb3F0c6EeadF30Ef1D3Eb997108E976351feB, deployed 2026-07-13) and PonsV2LaunchFactory (0x7E1EAbd52Ae29598e6483F72dCf1a70b14284dB8, deployed 2026-07-30), and it collects fees into two escrow contracts rather than one. PONSTR is Pons's own strategy token and trades only on a v3 pool, with no hook of its own. The distinction matters throughout this paper: Uniswap v3 pools cannot run custom code on a trade, so a v3 pool is structurally hookless and can never pay holders, while v4 pools can attach a hook and may or may not have one.

The two projects are joined by a routing integration, not by ownership. They can be treated as one meta, one shared narrative on one chain. They must never be treated as one project, and any claim that migrates from Pons's launch volume to INDEX's yield engine is doing exactly that.

Pons is the ecosystem's clearest revenue proxy because its fee take is directly measurable on-chain. Platform fees measured $444,113 on 2026-08-01 and about $331,000 per day by 2026-08-05, and falling. The launchpad has produced 245,327 token launches since 2026-07-13, peaking at 21,488 launches on 2026-07-21, the same day fees peaked.

Layer 3: Sinjoh, the routing and burn layer

Sinjoh is a family of fee-router and collector contracts that route a slice of Pons activity into buying and burning INDEX. The machinery is real and executes on-chain. Cumulative burns measured 40,353.0134 INDEX on 2026-08-02 and were independently re-measured at 40,392.5470 INDEX from the burn address transfer history, a 0.1 percent variance between two methods.

Disclosed structural risk. There is no factory contract behind Sinjoh. Every clone was deployed as an individual top-level transaction directly from one operator externally-owned account (EOA), 0x3d58E42d3a920dE4C1F71EE041c7eBb82ee23f49. Nine clones landed on 2026-07-30, a tenth on 2026-08-03, an eleventh on 2026-08-04: at least eleven verified fee router clones as of the 2026-08-05 census. The same operator address had roughly 27 total contract deploys by 2026-08-06, most not yet identified, so the true clone count is likely higher. An externally-owned account is a plain wallet controlled by whoever holds its private key, with no multisig, no timelock, and no contract logic constraining it. The entire burn layer therefore depends on one key. This also means any published clone count is a dated enumeration, not a live one, and burn tracking undercounts whenever the operator deploys again without the tracker being updated.

Layer 4: Indices, the treasury builder

Indices is theindex.finance's own dividend-basket product, surfaced in its UI as Baskets, Build, Coins and Portfolio. It runs on two contracts: StockTreasury (0x1604Ff11dFeAaC437077aEDA2FA492ac9EC804dF, V1, ETH-denominated, deployed 2026-07-03) and USDGBuyerDistributorV2 (0x39ADB8acD07427D338b5f1AfAb436A04AbFdB7c4, V2, USDG-denominated, deployed 2026-07-28). USDG is a dollar stablecoin, a token designed to hold a $1 price, so V2 spends dollars where V1 spent ETH.

This is a correction to a widely assumed model. Indices is not a per-user basket factory and never was. It runs exactly two shared, protocol-owned baskets. There is no multi-tenant path in the verified ABI evidence. Every "baskets created" figure in this paper is therefore a count of StockAdded events, meaning stocks registered into those two fixed baskets, not baskets built by users. V1 holds 6 stocks, V2 holds 18, for a cumulative 24.

Ownership is settled, not assumed. Task 2's contract inventory reached VERDICT: VERIFIED on the question of whether rwa.wtf and theindex.finance are the same team, and its allowed phrasing is quoted in full in section 6, leg 4.

Context frame: why this meta was cheap to build

Uniswap v4 shipped hooks, which let a pool run custom logic on every swap. On Robinhood Chain that capability is not exotic; it is the default. Of 246,379 total v4 pools on the chain, 97,020 carry a hook, with the first hooked deploy on 2026-06-19. Building a custom fee market went from a protocol engineering project to a days-long build.

That cuts both ways, and the second edge is sharper than the first. Cheap to build means cheap to clone, cheap to fork, and cheap to abandon. It also means a hook confers no moat. The board's own headline finding is the proof: VIRTUAL traded $24.3M and GME traded $13.8M over a trailing 7 days with exactly zero volume on their own hooked pools. Tokenized stocks show the identical pattern, a hooked pool that exists and sits idle while the volume trades somewhere else.

4. Bull Thesis, Stated Fairly

Five legs, stated as an advocate would state them, before any of them are tested. Each carries the verification verdict it was assigned in this project's verification workpack.

Leg 1: Orchestrator alignment. Robinhood is pushing tokenization hard and building its own chain. Its chief executive is personally engaged with what launches there. The claim as it circulates is that he follows only two coins, and that this signals which experiments the orchestrator wants to succeed. Verdict: UNVERIFIED. This is an unverified narrative and is labeled as such everywhere it appears below.

Leg 2: Product velocity as recurring income sources. The team does not ship one product and stop. Buybacks, the Pons V2 launchpad generation, and the Indices treasury builder all arrived inside a few weeks, each one adding another recurring income source to the same holder base. Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.

Leg 3: Reward-switching as an on-chain portfolio manager. The payout asset is a contract parameter, not a constant. A protocol that can rotate what it pays holders in is a portfolio manager that happens to live on-chain, and that is a far larger product than a dividend token. Verdict: roadmap claim, tested against contract evidence below.

Leg 4: Permissionless index creation and rwa.wtf expansion. If anyone can create an index, the product stops being one basket and becomes a venue. rwa.wtf extends the same team's surface from dividends into real-world-asset trading. Verdict: VERIFIED on team identity, tested on the permissionless claim below.

Leg 5: Institutionalization. A team that incorporates is a team building for a multi-year horizon, one that can raise venture capital, sign counterparties, and survive its own token's drawdowns. Verdict: PARTIALLY VERIFIED.

Reading the table, metric by metric

Daily holder inflows. This is the whole engine in one number: the dollars per day flowing from traders into stock purchases for holders. It is modeled, not measured, because the hook's own fee transfers are not decoded on Dune. The model applies a 3 percent take to the hooked pool's trailing volume. The direction is what matters here, and the direction is a fall from about $18,300 per day to about $2,600 per day in nine days. The structural point beneath it is that there is no insolvency mode in this design, only a volume-decay mode. Traders pay holders directly, so the yield dies exactly as fast as the tape does.

Payout yield per $1,000 per day. The same engine, normalized to something a finance reader can price. It answers what a $1,000 position earns per day at current activity. At $0.875 per day, a $1,000 position earns roughly 2.6 percent of its notional per month in stock payouts, which sounds strong until you note it was $2.947 per day at the July 17 to 29 median and that the yield falls as the tape falls. An independent census read on 2026-08-02 put it at $0.41 per day against a $2.55 healthy-period median. The two methods disagree on level and agree on direction, which is the honest way to report it.

Hook share of INDEX volume. The most important and least intuitive metric in the set. The hook only pays holders on volume that crosses the hooked pool. Volume that trades anywhere else, on the hookless v3 pool or on any other venue, generates zero holder income while still moving the price and still appearing in headline volume figures. At 14.2 percent, roughly six of every seven dollars of INDEX volume pay holders nothing. This is the metric that explains why the memo's original July 24 trigger fired on the wrong number: headline volume looked alive while yield-carrying volume was already collapsing.

Registered payout holders. The count of wallets eligible to receive payouts. Reported here as a balance-threshold proxy, wallets holding at or above 10,000 INDEX, because the actual payout registry is off-chain and cannot be queried directly. That proxy reads 2,514 on 2026-08-05 against 2,379 at the July 24 opt-in census. The two use different definitions, so this is directional evidence of a holder base that has not fled, not a clean like-for-like growth number, and the framework table flags it as not a clean trigger hit for exactly that reason.

Pons cohort 7d survival. Group every token Pons launched in one week, then ask how many still trade at all a week or more later. For the 2026-07-13 cohort, 52,429 launches produced 5.06 percent still trading 24 days on. Younger cohorts read far higher (41.2 percent for 07-27, 62.7 percent for 08-03) for the boring reason that they have not had time to die yet. Reading a young cohort as improvement is the standard error here, and this framework does not make it.

Indices stocks added. Cumulative StockAdded events across the two shared baskets, currently 24 and flat for nine straight days. This metric's definition was corrected during implementation, from a per-user basket count to a stocks-registered count, because the contracts do not implement a per-user path. Its trigger was adapted for the same reason and the adaptation is disclosed in the table itself rather than buried. The adapted trigger, at least one new stock added in a trailing week, is currently not met.

Treasury buys per round. The dollars of USDG actually spent buying tokenized stocks in one payout round, about $2,457 in the latest completed round. A round is bounded by the next round's start event, which matters because an in-progress round reads low simply for being unfinished. 179 rounds have run since the V2 launch on 2026-07-28. The trend cell is honest about a gap: nine days of lumpy round-level data is not enough to call a three-round trend, and the framework records that as an open data gap rather than filling it with a number.

Lifecycle stage call: plateau to early unwind

Pay-you tokens move through recognizable stages: launch, a reflexive phase where rising price and rising activity feed each other, a plateau where activity flattens while the narrative keeps selling, and an unwind where activity and price fall together.

The call here is plateau to early unwind, moved further along that axis than the project's own starting baseline of "entering plateau" by data recorded since. Holder inflow is down about 86 percent from its 2026-07-24 peak. Payout yield per $1,000 has fallen from a $2.55 healthy median to $0.875 per day, with the independent census read disagreeing on level and agreeing on direction. Pons platform fees, the ecosystem's clearest measured revenue proxy, fell from $444,000 per day on 2026-08-01 to about $331,000 per day on 2026-08-05.

Nothing here confirms a full unwind. Younger Pons cohorts still show real survival and Indices treasury buys keep executing round after round, with 179 rounds recorded and no sign of the product stopping. The newer product lines function. The core yield engine decays.

6. Leg-by-Leg Verdict

This section resolves the tension in the paper directly. The bull thesis in section 4 is coherent and its machinery is largely real. The desk's standing read on this asset is the opposite of bullish: exit stands, watch-only below a $10M market cap, a read written on 2026-07-24 and confirmed by a 2026-08-02 census that found market cap down 44 percent, liquidity around $628K, and the unwind trigger tripped.

Both can be true, and the resolution is not a compromise. The bull thesis describes mechanisms. The bearish read describes flows. Every mechanism in the thesis is real or partly real. Almost none of them currently carries scale. That gap, mechanism verified and scale unsupported, is the single finding this paper contributes, and it repeats across four of five legs.

Leg 1: Orchestrator alignment

The verification verdict on this leg is UNVERIFIED, and its allowed phrasing is quoted verbatim:

Use in paper: unverified narrative. Vlad Tenev is documented following Robinhood-chain meme accounts (CASHCAT, Pons founder @MEADGod, WLFI advisor Ogle), but no evidence surfaced in this sweep that he has followed or acknowledged INDEX or theindex.finance specifically, and the "only follows two coins" claim could not be traced to a source.

The honest framing is narrow. The general pattern of orchestrator engagement with this chain's meme economy is documented in mainstream coverage. The specific claim that carries the bull leg, that INDEX is one of a tiny set of anointed experiments, is an unverified narrative and was not traceable to any source. It also fails on its own terms: the follows that are documented already number more than two and do not include INDEX. Because X does not expose a full following list without an authenticated session, this is an absence of found evidence rather than a proven negative, and should be read that way in both directions.

An unverified narrative is not a small problem when it sits at the top of a thesis stack, because the other four legs are all evaluated by readers who have already accepted it.

Leg 2: Product velocity as recurring income sources

The verification verdict is PARTIALLY VERIFIED, quoted verbatim:

Use in paper: verified as fact with citation, framed as a mechanism-vs-scale split. The Pons launchpad and its PONS-to-INDEX burn integration are real and live on-chain (Dune, https://dune.com/czr/pons-launchpad-health; memo-index.md), but the promoted scale claims are not supported by measured data: Pons platform fees measured $444,113 on 2026-08-01 (cross-checked to DefiLlama's $388,464), not the $54 million per day figure attached to the launch-volume narrative, and cumulative INDEX burned through the PONS integration totaled about $280 all time as of the 2026-08-02 census, roughly three orders of magnitude below claims of tens of thousands of dollars burned per day.

Velocity is real. Three systems shipped inside one four-week window, Indices V1 on 2026-07-03 through V2 on 2026-07-28 and both Pons V2 and the Sinjoh burn routers on 2026-07-30, and all three execute on-chain. What fails is the conversion from velocity into income. A $54 million per day figure circulated and could not be traced to any sourced claim in that unit; the nearest public figures are a market cap near $54 million and a 54 percent share of chain activity, two different quantities that appear to have been conflated into a volume number upstream. Measured fees ran in the hundreds of thousands per day in the same window and have fallen since. The burn integration is the sharper case: real machinery, independently confirmed live, moving about $280 of value all time.

This is what a late-plateau narrative injection looks like. A genuine new mechanism ships, the narrative attaches a scale claim to it, and the measured throughput arrives three orders of magnitude short.

Leg 3: Reward-switching as an on-chain portfolio manager

This leg was not assigned a verification verdict because it is a roadmap claim, not a factual one. It is tested against contract evidence instead.

The capability exists and is disclosed: the owner can change the payout asset, the supported assets, and keeper permissions. That is precisely the permission a reward-switching product would require.

It is also precisely the permission an exit would require. The same key that could rotate holders from NVDA into something better could rotate them into something worthless, and nothing on-chain constrains which. Presenting an unconstrained admin power as a product roadmap inverts its risk sign. Until a switching mechanism ships with a stated rule for who decides and how often, the correct classification of this leg is a disclosed centralization risk that the bull case has relabeled as a feature.

Leg 4: Permissionless index creation and rwa.wtf ecosystem expansion

The team-identity half is VERIFIED, quoted verbatim:

Use in paper: verified as fact with citation. rwa.wtf and theindex.finance are operated by the same entity, The Index Finance, Inc.: rwa.wtf's own Terms of Use and Privacy Policy (fetched from its production JS bundle, /assets/index-Bwl_EcEo.js) name The Index Finance, Inc. as rwa.wtf's legal operator with contact admin@theindex.finance, and on-chain, the same deployer EOA (0x89562Eb8979dB1E85A01E85120BFD6A7C47a39cb, Robinhood Chain, verified via robinhoodchain.blockscout.com) created the INDEX token and its full basket-distribution stack (LpLock, StockTreasury, SwapRouter, USDGBuyerDistributorV2) between 2026-07-03 and 2026-07-28. rwa.wtf itself runs its perpetuals trading through Lighter's third-party infrastructure rather than deploying its own Robinhood-chain contracts. The Indices/Baskets mechanism (theindex.finance's "3% ETH tax buys tokenized stocks, every 15 minutes" dividend feature) is a real, live, team-deployed system, and its contracts belong in the same board-9 family as the previously audited INDEX hook infrastructure.

So the expansion half of this leg holds: one team, two surfaces, both live. The permissionless half does not. The verified contracts implement two shared, protocol-owned baskets and no multi-tenant creation path. There is no factory and no public creation function. "Permissionless index creation" describes a product that has not been deployed.

This distinction is also why the framework's own metric had to be redefined mid-project. A baskets_created_total of 24 sounds like 24 user-created indices. It is 24 stocks registered into two operator-controlled baskets, and it has not moved in nine days.

One further note that cuts against reading rwa.wtf as chain expansion: its perps order book runs on Lighter's third-party zero-knowledge rollup infrastructure, not on Robinhood-chain contracts the team deployed. It expands the team's surface. It does not expand the chain's.

Leg 5: Institutionalization

The verification verdict is PARTIALLY VERIFIED, quoted verbatim:

Use in paper: verified as fact with citation, with one disclosed gap. rwa.wtf's own bundled legal text (Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, pulled from its production JS bundle at https://rwa.wtf/assets/index-Bwl_EcEo.js) names "The Index Finance, Inc." as the operator, with contact admin@theindex.finance and a Wilmington, Delaware registered-agent address, establishing that the team operates under a named Delaware-registered-agent corporate entity. This was not confirmed against the Delaware Division of Corporations' own registry (file number, formation date), since that registry requires an interactive form submission this research could not complete with keyless tools.

This is the strongest-evidenced leg in the stack and also the weakest inference. The evidence is first-party and specific: an operator's own production legal text naming an entity, a registered agent, and a contact address is not a rumor. The inference the bull case draws from it is much larger than the evidence supports. Incorporation is a routine administrative step available to anyone who files. It is a necessary condition for raising capital and signing counterparties, not evidence that either has happened. No raise, cap table, or named institutional counterparty surfaced in this research.

Read correctly, this leg upgrades the team from anonymous to identified. That is genuinely meaningful for counterparty risk. It says nothing about runway.

Reconciling with the standing desk read

The desk's standing read is exit stands, watch-only below a $10M market cap, stated as positioning language with no sizes disclosed. Nothing in this leg-by-leg pass overturns it, because the legs that survive verification are all mechanism legs, and the read is a flow read.

What would overturn it is written down in the framework table and repeated in the fourth column above. The clearest single trigger is holder inflow rebuilding above $10,000 per day and holding there for a week, because that one metric is the yield engine and every other bull leg routes through it. Second is hook share above 50 percent, which would mean the volume finally sits where it pays holders. A prior re-rate condition from the original memo remains on the list: volume rebuilding above $1M per day with buyer-majority uniques at 1.5 to 1 or better.

7. Risks

Inflow dependency. This is the defining risk and it is structural, not incidental. Holders are paid out of trader flow, so yield is a direct function of volume with no buffer, no reserve, and no accrual. There is no insolvency mode here, only a decay mode, and the decay is currently measured at about 86 percent in nine days. A dividend that falls with the tape offers no support to the tape.

Single-EOA operator concentration. The Sinjoh burn layer was deployed entirely from one externally-owned account with no factory, no multisig, and no timelock. Separately, the INDEX token and its full basket-distribution stack trace to a single other deployer key. Both subsystems are one-key systems. Two operational wallets across two subsystems is a normal team pattern; the risk is not that there are two keys, it is that there is no constraint on either.

Payout-asset admin power. The owner can change the payout asset, the supported assets, and keeper permissions, and these are disclosed rather than hidden. Bull leg 3 depends on this power being used well. Risk analysis requires assuming it could be used badly, and there is no on-chain constraint that prevents it.

Regulatory surface of tokenized-stock payouts. A protocol that pays token holders in tokenized equities is distributing something that looks like a security to holders who did not go through an onboarding process. The tokenized stocks are sourced through third-party infrastructure, which adds a dependency the protocol does not control. This paper takes no view on how any regulator would treat it. The surface exists and it is larger than that of a token that pays nothing.

Lifecycle mechanics. The pay-you class has a characteristic arc, and this asset is measurably in the plateau-to-early-unwind band of it. Late in that arc, new mechanism announcements arrive faster than earlier, which reads as accelerating product velocity and is better read as narrative injection. The Pons integration burn claim is a worked example from this ecosystem: real machinery, real deployment, and about $280 of measured throughput against a claimed run rate three orders of magnitude higher.

Measurement risk in this paper's own numbers. Holder inflow is modeled at a 3 percent take, not measured, because the hook's fee deltas are not decoded. USD volume figures on the hook boards are floors, since roughly a quarter of matched hooked swaps carry a null price and are excluded. Sinjoh clone counts are dated enumerations that undercount whenever the operator deploys again. Registered holder counts compare an on-chain balance threshold against an off-chain opt-in registry. Every one of these biases is disclosed on the live boards, and a reader should treat the metric directions as more reliable than the metric levels.

8. Conclusion

The INDEX bull thesis is better built than most in its class. Its mechanisms are real, verified at the contract level, and mostly do what they claim. That is a genuinely low bar cleared, and the majority of pay-you tokens do not clear it.

The thesis fails on scale, not on truth, and it fails the same way in four different places. Product velocity is real and the income it produces is three orders of magnitude below what was claimed for it. The team is real and identified, and incorporation is being read as funding. The index product is real and it is two operator-controlled baskets, not a permissionless venue. The reward-switching capability is real and is currently an admin key with no rule attached. Only the orchestrator leg fails on truth, and it fails as an unverified narrative that no source in this sweep supports.

The engine underneath all five legs is holder inflow, and holder inflow is down about 86 percent from its peak with payout yield per $1,000 down from a $2.55 healthy median to $0.875 per day, two methods disagreeing on level and agreeing on direction, and only 14.2 percent of volume touching the pool that pays. The lifecycle call is plateau to early unwind. The standing desk stance, exit stands and watch-only below a $10M market cap, is unchanged by this analysis.

The useful output of a thesis test is not the verdict, it is the trigger list. Five numbers, written down in advance, decide whether this read is wrong: holder inflow above $10,000 per day sustained a week, hook share above 50 percent, payout yield at or above $2.55 per day, Pons cohort survival above 15 percent, and at least one new stock added to a basket in a trailing week. All five are live on public boards. Any reader can check them without asking the author whether the call has changed.

Methodology notes

  1. Holder inflow is modeled, not measured. A 3 percent hook take is applied to the hooked pool's volume, because the hook's own fee deltas are not decoded on Dune. The pool's own swap fee is a separate 1.00 percent, read from its Initialize event. The two are different quantities and the distinction is carried on the live board.
  2. Two hook-share numbers exist and they measure different things. The all-venue share (14.2 percent) compares the hooked pool against every Robinhood-chain venue trading INDEX. The two-pool share on the holder-revenue board compares the hooked v4 pool against the one hookless v3 pool and reads higher. This paper uses the all-venue figure throughout.
  3. USD volume figures are floors. Pricing comes from joining each decoded v4 Swap event to its own dex.trades row. About 97.6 percent of hooked swaps match a trade row and about 74.8 percent of those carry a nonnull USD amount, so roughly a quarter of matched swaps are unpriced and excluded. True volume is somewhat higher, which biases hook share slightly downward.
  4. Registered holder counts use a proxy. The payout registry is off-chain. The on-chain figure counts wallets at or above the 10,000 INDEX threshold and excludes unlabelled contract addresses. It is not a like-for-like match to the July 24 opt-in census.
  5. The Indices metrics were redefined mid-project. baskets_created_total counts StockAdded events across two shared protocol baskets, not per-user basket creations, and its bull trigger was adapted accordingly. basket_weight_pct is a realized spend share on a 0 to 100 scale for the most recent completed round, bounded by the next round's start, not a declared target weight.
  6. Pons fee measurement sums three legs. Native ETH (schema-correct, measures zero in practice), WETH, and token sweeps into two escrow contracts. Dev-buy value passing through the launch factories is deliberately excluded because it is not fee revenue. The series cross-checks against DefiLlama's dailyFees within the board's stated plus or minus 20 percent tolerance.
  7. Sinjoh clone counts are dated, not live. At least eleven verified fee router clones as of the 2026-08-05 census; the same operator address had roughly 27 total contract deploys by 2026-08-06, most not yet identified, so the true clone count is likely higher.
